## The pattern

A funding round closes, an enterprise customer sends a security questionnaire, and
within a month there is a [[siem]] deployment nobody asked for. Six months later
the alert queue has four thousand unread items and the honest answer to "would you
have caught it?" is no.

The tool was not the problem. The sequence was.

## Buy the owner before the tool

Detection tooling converts raw events into alerts. Alerts are only worth what the
response to them is worth. If nobody owns the queue, you have paid to generate a
documented record that you were warned - which is materially worse than having no
record at all when it comes to a breach investigation.

So the order is: someone owns it, then you tune it, then you scale ingest. A SIEM
bought in the other order is shelfware with a monthly invoice.

## What we would do instead at twenty people

- Turn on cloud-native audit logging everywhere and set retention. It is nearly
  free and it is the evidence you will want later.
- Deploy [[edr]] on every laptop and server. It produces useful alerts with minimal
  tuning and gives you a containment button.
- Write down who looks at alerts, when, and what they are authorised to do without
  waking anyone up.
- Only then evaluate a SIEM, sized against the log sources you actually decided
  were worth correlating.
